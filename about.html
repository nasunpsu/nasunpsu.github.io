About

Senior UX Researcher · Google · PhD, Information Technology (HCI focus)

I study how people coordinate through software.

That question has stayed fairly constant across work that looks, on the surface, quite different. My dissertation at Penn State was about distance learners — people enrolled in the same program for years who never met each other, and often weren't sure a community existed at all. I built the instruments to measure whether they felt a community existed, then built a Slack bot and a web app to try to change the answer. Two of those studies went to CHI, one received a Best Paper Honorable Mention, and the whole arc is written up as a case study.

At Google I've spent seven years on variations of the same problem. On Core Data, the users were internal teams depending on crawling, processing, indexing, and analytics infrastructure they hadn't built themselves. On the Chat Platform, they were third-party developers deciding whether a platform was worth committing to. On the Identity and Groups Platform, they were IT admins deciding who — inside the company and outside it — should be able to reach what. Most recently it's been generative AI: I led the focus-group study behind Gemini at Work (DIS 2025), which found that AI at work didn't simply make people faster. It changed which parts of a job people chose to keep for themselves.

The methods change a great deal; the reason doesn't. The part I care about is where research actually moves a decision — the roadmap that gets rewritten, the launch that waits.

How I work

Most of my projects start before there's an interface to evaluate, so the method usually has to be built rather than picked off a shelf. Roughly half my work is foundational and qualitative, half is quantitative, and the interesting part is usually where the two disagree.

Qualitative Foundational interviews, contextual inquiry, focus groups, diary and field studies, observational usability, think-aloud, co-design workshops
Quantitative Survey design, MaxDiff, TURF, longitudinal satisfaction benchmarking, significance testing, experiment design, log and telemetry analysis, regression, factor analysis
Programs Standing measurement systems, cross-org shared metrics, research strategy across multi-quarter roadmaps
Currently growing Product strategy and business acumen

Leading the practice

Some of the work I'm proudest of isn't a study. I lead my team's recurring research critique sessions, organize an academic speaker series that brings university researchers into conversation with product teams, and mentor both researchers inside the company and doctoral students outside it. I've onboarded colleagues onto quantitative survey tooling, guided a technical writing team through a developer documentation survey that drew more than 1,250 responses, and been invited to present research to partner product teams across the suite.

I'm also lead author on a case study about what actually makes a UX research community healthy — which is the same question as my dissertation, pointed at my own field.

Community in practice

I spent four years studying how community forms among people who never meet in person, and then kept doing the work by hand. I organize book clubs, and around International Women's Day I run a three-part series on women's empowerment alongside educational sessions on women's health. I also captain a team in the Bay Area Industrial Tennis League, which is community building with a scoreboard.

The research version of me measures whether shared identity can be made visible. The other version just books the room.
