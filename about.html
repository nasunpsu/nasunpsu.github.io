About

Senior UX Researcher · Google · PhD, Information Technology (HCI focus)

I study how people coordinate through software.

That question has stayed fairly constant across work that looks, on the surface, quite different. My dissertation at Penn State was about distance learners — people enrolled in the same program for years who never met each other, and often weren't sure a community existed at all. Two of those studies went to CHI, and one received a Best Paper Honorable Mention.

At Google I've spent seven years on variations of the same problem. On Core Data, the users were internal teams depending on crawling, processing, indexing, and analytics infrastructure they hadn't built themselves. On the Chat Platform, they were third-party developers deciding whether a platform was worth committing to. On the Identity and Groups Platform, they were IT admins deciding who — inside the company and outside it — should be able to reach what. Most recently it's been generative AI: I led the focus-group study behind Gemini at Work (DIS 2025), which found that AI at work didn't simply make people faster. It changed which parts of a job people chose to keep for themselves.

The methods change a great deal; the reason doesn't. The part I care about is where research actually moves a decision — the roadmap that gets rewritten, the launch that waits.
