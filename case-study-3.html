Gemini for Workspace: Understanding Enterprise GenAI Adoption

How do knowledge workers actually use generative AI day to day — and what does that mean for product strategy? Published at DIS 2025.

RoleLead Researcher
MethodsStructured focus groups, cross-job-family sampling, qualitative synthesis
SampleN = 37 knowledge workers across 7 job families
ScopeGmail, Docs, Drive, Slides, Meet
PublishedACM DIS 2025

The Problem

Generative AI was moving into everyday knowledge work fast, but the product organization lacked a grounded understanding of how it actually changed people's workflows — versus how leadership assumed it would. Enterprise GenAI strategy needed to be built on evidence, not extrapolation from consumer AI usage patterns.

My Approach

I designed structured focus groups spanning seven distinct job families and recruited 37 knowledge workers to evaluate Gemini for Workspace across Gmail, Docs, Drive, Slides, and Meet. The cross-job-family design was deliberate — a workflow accelerant for one role can be a distraction for another, and the strategy needed to account for that variation rather than average it away.

What I Found

The research uncovered specific patterns of workflow acceleration and, notably, shifts in how tasks got allocated between people and AI — not a uniform productivity bump, but a reorganization of who does what.

Impact: Findings directly informed GenAI product strategy for Workspace, and the work was accepted for publication at the 2025 ACM Designing Interactive Systems Conference (DIS '25) — a competitive venue with a 25% acceptance rate. Read the paper on Google Scholar.

What Happened Next

The task-allocation findings in particular reframed internal conversations about what "AI productivity" should even be measured against — shifting focus from raw time saved toward where human judgment still needed to stay in the loop.
