Scaling the API Happiness Program (HaTS)

Building the first standardized way to measure developer happiness across Workspace Platform's APIs — and turning it into a cross-org operating rhythm.

RoleProgram Lead, Senior UX Researcher
MethodsHappiness Tracking Survey (HaTS) design, quantitative analysis, program design
ScaleScaled from 5 to 33 APIs
DomainWorkspace Platform · Tech Writer & DevRel orgs

The Problem

Workspace Platform had dozens of APIs and no standardized, comparable way to know how developers actually felt using them. Without a shared measure, the Tech Writer and DevRel organizations had no common language for prioritizing where to invest — decisions were being made on anecdote rather than evidence.

My Approach

I pioneered an API-specific adaptation of Google's Happiness Tracking Survey (HaTS) methodology — a validated quantitative instrument — and piloted it on a small set of APIs before scaling. The program design deliberately built in comparability across APIs and over time, so scores could be tracked as a trend, not just a one-off snapshot.

What I Found

Once live across a wider set of APIs, the data revealed clear, ranked variation in developer happiness — and specific, recurring pain points that individual APIs shared, which pointed to systemic fixes rather than one-off patches.

Impact: The program scaled from 5 to 33 APIs and became the basis for new shared OKRs between the Tech Writer and DevRel organizations — turning a research method into a standing cross-org measurement system.

What Happened Next

HaTS scores became a recurring input into prioritization conversations across two previously disconnected orgs, giving both a shared, quantitative way to argue for investment — the kind of infrastructure that keeps generating value long after the original study ends.
