Core Data Product Usability & Foundational Research

Getting a core data product ready for General Availability by finding — and fixing — where users got stuck.

RoleSenior UX Researcher
MethodsUsability testing, foundational research, sentiment tracking
DomainCore Data, Google Workspace
PeriodOct 2019 – Mar 2022

The Problem

Ahead of General Availability, a core data product needed to prove it could reliably get real users to task completion — not just pass internal review. Usability gaps at this stage are expensive: they either delay launch or ship as support burden and churn risk once the product is public.

My Approach

I ran foundational and evaluative usability research in parallel — combining task-based usability testing with a user sentiment measure, so the team could see not just whether people completed tasks, but how the experience felt while they did it. This paired view made it possible to distinguish "technically works but frustrating" from "genuinely broken," which changed how engineering prioritized fixes.

What I Found

Testing surfaced specific friction points in the task flow that were quietly suppressing both completion rates and user confidence — the kind of issues that don't show up in log data until you watch someone hit them in real time.

Impact: Research-driven fixes lifted Task Success Rate by +37% and user sentiment by +8 points, clearing the path for the product to successfully reach General Availability.

What Happened Next

The product shipped to General Availability on the strength of this research — a direct example of usability work functioning as a launch gate, not just a nice-to-have polish pass.
